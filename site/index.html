01 — the problem you already have

One coding agent was a superpower. Five is a second job.

Each agent is useful alone. Coordinating several becomes its own job.

8:12 · coffee

You skim one brief, not ten sessions

11:40 · heads-down

Your desks work while you do your own thing

1:15 · lunch

4:30 · new work lands

A fresh desk that already knows your project

03 — what you get

Three ideas do the whole job.

01

Flotillas — your domains, run for you

Group work by domain. Each flotilla owns its desks and can start or retire with the work instead of becoming another flat backlog.

02

Memex — it remembers, and it learns

Each desk carries conventions, decisions, and prior attempts across sessions through memex, rather than starting from zero.

03

A chief of staff — one point of contact

One coordinator routes intent, gathers replies, and tells you what happened in plain language.

05 — how it actually works

One hub you talk to. Your desks around it. Everything on the record.

  1. you talk to one agent

    Chat is the whole interface

    Give the coordinator an outcome from your laptop or phone.

  2. it reaches your real sessions

    Delivered, and confirmed

    Each desk pulls durable work before acting; acknowledgments and supersession remain inspectable.

  3. state and receipts survive

    Continue without losing the record

    Handoffs preserve context, and approval-sensitive work still stops for you.

Six registered drivers: Claude Code, Codex, Grok, OpenCode, Pi, and aider.

When auto-switch is enabled, eligible non-approval-sensitive desks currently running Claude Code can move to another configured harness in their declared fallback chain after a detected provider rate limit, carrying a durable handoff into the replacement session.

Five dashboard destinations: Conversations, Goals, Issues, Parade, and combined R&D. Goals maps the fleet; Issues exposes its work; Parade and R&D keep reports and decisions readable.

Built for continuity: stable seat identities, goals-as-data, durable delivery receipts, operator interrupt, worktree lifecycle, installable doctrine, and a private/public boundary.
